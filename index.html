Lecturer & Critical Media Studies Researcher

Welcome!

I am Angelica Martinez Ochoa, a researcher, writer, and lecturer whose work moves across art, technology, and critical media studies to examine how digital infrastructures and global markets are reshaping the lives of women, mothers, and those who care for one another. My research explores the intersections of transnational labor markets, platform capitalism, and feminist science and technology studies, tracing how the design of digital platforms mediates motherhood, work, and social reproduction.

Through the lens of critical media theory, I study how emerging technologies, from gig-care platforms to generative AI, are redefining the meaning of labor, care, and value in contemporary life. I situate these transformations within broader histories of welfare, globalization, and post-industrial economies, revealing how the global care economy operates as both a site of extraction and resistance.

Ultimately, I am interested in how technological design, discourse, and international cooperation in a multipolar world can help us imagine more equitable technological futures, ones that sustain all of us, and especially the mothers and women whose labor continues to hold our societies together.

Digital Portfolio & Projects

A selection of digital media, design, and research-driven projects that demonstrate technical and creative application of critical theory.

Motherhood, Labor and Digital Technologies

Visual Essays

A critique of the entanglement of motherhood and work, developed in collaboration with Catalina Alzate. Analyzes digital technologies like smart screens and apps that mediate mothering.

Mothers and the Gig Economy

Posters

Commentary on the “Uberization of motherhood” through case studies of Gig Economy platforms that position mothers as human capital.

Digital Portfolio of Students' Work

Highlighting exemplary digital, critical design, and analytical projects from courses I have taught, demonstrating student application of critical media concepts and technical proficiency.

Technoculture: Speculative Design, Dear AI Letters & Tarot Cards of Tech

A curated collection of student projects blending design fiction (to critique algorithmic bias), reflective letters to AI (to explore ethical visions for the technology), and critical analysis using the Tarot Cards of Tech (to prompt reflection on technological impact).

Features the "Instagram Campaign" and the non-linear "Community Stories" project (using Twine), demonstrating critical media art and narrative design for social commentary.

Student-led projects that analyze global films through the lens of critical media theory. The projects focus on creating **video essays** and written scripts that categorize cinema based on transnational flows and post-colonial narratives.
